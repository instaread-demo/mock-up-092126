Narnia Logo Gives Netflix A Fresh Start For A Familiar Fantasy World

By Aleksei Titov Sep 19, 2026

Some movie titles arrive with decades of visual baggage attached.

Narnia is one of them. The word immediately brings back snowy forests, old books, previous films and one extremely famous lion. Netflix could easily lean on all of that nostalgia for its new adaptation.

Instead, its first Narnia logo feels more like an introduction.

The identity has been revealed for Greta Gerwig’s The Magician’s Nephew, accompanied by the line “A universe begins.” It’s a fitting choice for the only major Narnia story that actually shows that universe beginning.

The logo is built around the NARNIA name itself. Large, prominent lettering carries the franchise, while The Magician’s Nephew sits beneath it as the title of this particular story. There is no need to cram a wardrobe, lamppost, lion or other familiar piece of Narnia imagery into the mark.

The name is recognizable enough.

That gives the design room to establish something new around it.

It’s an important distinction because Netflix isn’t beginning where most Narnia adaptations begin. The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe was the first book C. S. Lewis published and remains by far the best-known story in the series. The Magician’s Nephew came much later in publication order but takes place first chronologically.

It is Narnia before Narnia becomes familiar.

That makes it an unusually smart place to build a new screen identity.

There is no previous movie version of The Magician’s Nephew hanging over the production. The story has never received a major film adaptation, so Gerwig gets familiar characters, places and mythology without having to recreate a movie audiences already know scene by scene.

Netflix gets the same freedom with the branding.

The animated version of the logo pushes the idea further. Rather than functioning only as lettering for a poster, it can appear as part of the creation of this new Narnia, which suits a story where the birth of a world is central to the plot.

Motion also matters for much more practical reasons.

This logo will spend a lot of time on screens.

Netflix has already launched dedicated Narnia accounts across its major social platforms and updated the film’s online presence. The identity needs to work in trailers, short clips, profile graphics and tiny social posts long before it appears at the beginning of the finished movie.

But unusually for Netflix, it also has a much bigger screen waiting.

The Magician’s Nephew begins IMAX previews on February 10, 2027, before opening in theaters worldwide on February 12. It won’t arrive on Netflix until April 2.

For a company built around watching movies at home, that’s a remarkably generous theatrical window.

The contrast with earlier Netflix releases is striking. The Irishman spent less than a month in limited theatrical release before reaching the service. Glass Onion: A Knives Out Mystery received just a week in cinemas.

Narnia gets close to three months.

That means its logo has to do something many Netflix movie identities rarely get the chance to do: behave like the branding for a genuine theatrical franchise.

It needs to look convincing on a giant IMAX screen, not simply inside a streaming menu.

Fantasy has produced some of the strongest movie logos precisely because these franchises benefit from having one stable visual element while everything around them grows more complicated. Harry Potter could change locations, characters and poster styles while its unmistakable lettering kept the films connected. The Lord of the Rings did something similar with a title treatment that became shorthand for the entire cinematic world.

Narnia has the potential for the same kind of recognition.

The word itself is already famous enough.

Netflix just needs to make its version of it stick.

The choice of The Magician’s Nephew helps because this isn’t simply the first installment in release order. The story provides an actual visual starting point for the franchise.

Before the White Witch rules Narnia, before four children arrive through a wardrobe and before the lamppost becomes an instantly recognizable landmark, there is the creation of the world.

Aslan sings it into existence.

Gerwig has described that idea as one of the things that stayed with her after first reading the book as a child: a cosmic lion creating an entire universe through music.

It is a wonderfully strange image, and much more interesting territory for a new adaptation than simply trying to make the wardrobe entrance impressive again.

The logo doesn’t attempt to illustrate it.

Good.

There will be a movie for that.

Instead, the identity stays broad enough to belong to Narnia as a whole. That becomes important if Netflix’s long-planned adaptations expand beyond this film. Individual subtitles can change while NARNIA remains the recognizable piece holding everything together.

The cast certainly suggests that Netflix is treating the project at franchise scale.

Carey Mulligan, Daniel Craig, Emma Mackey, Ciarán Hinds and Meryl Streep are involved, while David McKenna and Beatrice Campbell take the young leading roles. Combined with Gerwig and the extended theatrical release, it is difficult to mistake The Magician’s Nephew for a routine streaming production.

The branding shouldn’t feel like one either.

And it doesn’t need to shout.

That’s probably the most useful thing about the logo reveal. Narnia already has enough cultural recognition that an identity covered in fantasy decoration would be unnecessary. The clean emphasis on the name lets the film itself establish what Gerwig’s version of the world looks like.

The tagline handles the rest.

“A universe begins.”

Four words explain why Netflix is returning to an old fantasy series through one of its least adapted books.

The Narnia logo doesn’t have to promise that audiences are going back somewhere they remember.

This time, they’re getting there first.
